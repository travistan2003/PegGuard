PegGuard: Stablecoin Reserve Stress Tester

Will this stablecoin still be worth $1 when everyone wants out?
Team Bulge Track Technical (build) Category Consumer or B2B fintech application Stack Python · Streamlit · Plotly · pytest Repo see Canvas submission

1What does it do?

PegGuard takes a fiat-backed stablecoin's reserve portfolio and simulates a redemption run against it, day by day, to show whether the issuer can still pay every token $1 on time.

  1. Input reserves. The user picks a bundled portfolio (USDT and USDC compositions from their Q2 2026 reports, an SVB-style duration mismatch, the USDC/SVB weekend of March 2023, a narrow benchmark) or edits any line: asset class, amount, maturity and yield.
  2. Day 0: shocks. A parallel rate shock reprices bills as zero-coupon bonds and notes as coupon bonds (P = Σ C/(1+y)t + F/(1+y)n); gold, bitcoin and equities fall by chosen percentages; secured loans take a credit loss; a bank failure freezes part of the bank cash at an assumed recovery rate.
  3. Days 1-N: the run. Redemption requests arrive on a front-loaded schedule. The issuer pays $1 per token by selling through a liquidity waterfall (cash → repo → money market fund → bills → notes → gold → crypto → other → loans). Each asset can only sell a set share of itself per day and pays a fire-sale discount; requests it cannot meet are queued. Optional panic feedback (Diamond & Dybvig, 1983) speeds up the run once reserves fall below par.
  4. Output. A verdict (HOLDS LIQUIDITY STRAIN BELOW PAR BREAKS), the daily coverage ratio (reserves ÷ tokens), value per token, unmet redemptions, a loss breakdown and a plain-English narrative.

Around this core mechanic: a Breaking Point map (441 runs over rate shocks 0-500bp × run sizes 0-100%, giving the exact run size at which the peg fails), a Reserve Quality page (duration, a 1-20 day liquidity ladder, a simplified GENIUS Act / MAS eligibility check), a replay of USDC's March 2023 depeg and a live supply / peg monitor (DefiLlama). A command-line script reproduces any run, and 19 unit tests check the pricing and engine.

2Who is it for?

Mei, Head of Treasury at a Singapore Major Payment Institution that pays out B2B cross-border invoices to Indonesia, the Philippines and Vietnam. To settle 24/7, the firm pre-funds US$20-50m of USDC and USDT float.

Her pain point. Her risk committee wants (a) an exposure limit per stablecoin and (b) a contingency plan for a depeg. Today she has only monthly PDF attestations (a snapshot of what the reserves were, published weeks later) and qualitative ratings. Neither tells her under what conditions this coin stops paying $1 within the five business days MAS expects, or how much float she can safely hold. The risk is real: in March 2023 a firm holding USDC float watched it fall about 12% over a weekend while US bank rails were closed and redemption was impossible.

Secondary users: crypto exchange and custodian risk teams setting collateral haircuts on stablecoins; analysts and students learning how reserve design drives run risk.

3Why does it need to exist?

The gap

  • Stablecoins are now payment infrastructure, but their risk is assessed with static disclosures. USDT and USDC alone back about $257bn of tokens (Tether liabilities $183.6bn; Circle stablecoin holder deposits $72.9bn, 30 Jun 2026). StraitsX alone serves 150+ enterprises (Week 10). Yet holders get monthly or quarterly attestations of composition, not how the reserves behave under stress.
  • The failure modes are concrete and different for each coin. USDC's risk was bank concentration ($3.3bn of about $40bn at SVB). An SVB-style long-duration portfolio fails on rates. A USDT-style mix fails on crypto/gold prices and on liquidity: in our model its secured loans and gold mean only about 48% of tokens can be paid on day 1 and runs above 75% are paid late. One "backing ratio" number hides all of this.
  • Regulation defines safe reserves but does not stress them. The GENIUS Act (July 2025) and the MAS single-currency stablecoin framework (Aug 2023) list eligible assets (cash, short T-bills of 93 days or less, short repo, government MMFs) and require 100% backing and fast par redemption. Firms still hold coins that sit partly outside these rules (our check: GENIUS-eligible assets cover only about 77% of USDT-style tokens) and need their own view.
  • Existing tools look backwards. Dashboards track supply and price after the fact; rating agencies give qualitative scores. Neither lets a treasurer ask "what if rates rise 300bp and 30% redeem in five days?"

Why this approach

  • Deterministic, transparent scenario engine rather than a black-box prediction model: a risk committee must be able to audit every assumption. All liquidity and fire-sale assumptions are visible and editable, and the Breaking Point map shows how sensitive the conclusion is.
  • Borrows proven bank practice (liquidity stress tests and liquidity ladders) and applies it to stablecoins, using course tools: bond pricing and duration (Week 3), systematic vs idiosyncratic risk (Week 5), runs and stablecoins (Weeks 9-10).
  • Public data only, works offline. Issuer reports plus free APIs. No keys, no custody, no regulatory licence needed to run it.

Why someone would pay. The value is avoided loss. A 5% depeg on a US$30m float costs US$1.5m. A subscription costing about 1% of that, which also gives the risk committee a documented methodology, is an easy decision. Customers are easy to reach: licensed payment institutions in Singapore are a short list of known firms, and MAS and GENIUS reporting requirements give them a compliance reason to buy. Risk we would manage: issuers may offer to pay for "certified" results. We would keep the methodology public and never take issuer fees for scores.

Scope and trade-offs. Built in about four weeks around one reliable core mechanic. Deliberately left out: a secondary-market token price model, user accounts and alerts, yield-curve twists and stochastic simulation, and on-chain reserve proofs. Reserve presets are point-in-time and some maturities/yields are labelled assumptions. This is an educational prototype, not investment or legal advice.
